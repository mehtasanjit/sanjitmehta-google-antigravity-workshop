# Lecture Pulse Requirements

## The need

University lectures can make it difficult for students to ask questions as they arise. Some students are uncomfortable speaking in front of a large class, while limited lecture time prevents a lecturer from hearing from everyone.

Lecturers therefore receive an incomplete picture of student understanding. A quiet room does not necessarily mean that students understand the material, and confusion may only become visible later through assignments or examinations.

Lecture Pulse is intended to provide a simple, low-pressure way for students to communicate what they do not understand and for lecturers to identify the questions shared by the class.

## Users

### Lecturers

Lecturers need to:

- invite students to participate during a lecture without disrupting the lesson;
- see student questions as they are submitted;
- understand which questions or unclear concepts affect the most students;
- respond to important questions while they are still relevant; and
- distinguish questions that have been addressed from those requiring follow-up.

### Students

Students need to:

- join a lecture activity quickly from a phone, tablet, or computer;
- ask a question without having to speak in front of the class;
- participate without creating a permanent account;
- indicate that another student's question also affects them; and
- see whether a question has been acknowledged or answered.

## Required experience

1. A lecturer can start a question session for a lecture.
2. Students can join the active session using a short code.
3. Students can submit questions or identify concepts they find unclear.
4. Student participation can remain anonymous to the class.
5. Students can support an existing question instead of submitting a duplicate.
6. The lecturer can see questions and their level of student interest as the session progresses.
7. The lecturer can mark a question as answered.
8. The lecturer can close the session when the activity is complete.

## Goals

- Make it easier for students to ask for clarification.
- Give quieter students an equal opportunity to participate.
- Help lecturers recognize shared misunderstandings early.
- Use limited lecture time to address the questions with the greatest student interest.
- Create a clear feedback loop between students and lecturers.

## Initial scope

The first version is limited to live lecture questions and feedback. It does not need to support attendance, grading, assessments, student performance tracking, or learning-management-system integration.

## Success criteria

Lecture Pulse meets its initial need when:

- a lecturer can begin a session with minimal preparation;
- a student can join and submit a question in a short period of time;
- new questions and expressions of shared interest are visible to the lecturer during the lecture;
- students can participate without publicly identifying themselves; and
- the lecturer can clearly identify which questions have and have not been addressed.
