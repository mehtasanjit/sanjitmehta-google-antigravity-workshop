# Product Definition: Lecture Pulse

## Vision
Lecture Pulse is a real-time classroom engagement and feedback platform designed to give students a low-pressure channel to express confusion, submit questions, and signal comprehension during university lectures. By aggregating live feedback and highlighting shared questions, Lecture Pulse helps lecturers gauge class understanding instantaneously—ensuring no student is left behind even in large lecture halls.

## Target Users
- **Students**: Join active lecture sessions frictionlessly without login requirement using a simple join code or QR code. They can anonymously signal comprehension ("Slower", "Confused", "Got It") and submit/upvote questions in real time.
- **Lecturers & TAs**: Create and manage lecture sessions, monitor live pulse meters and top-voted student questions on a real-time dashboard, and review post-lecture summary reports.

## Core Features
1. **Session Management & Easy Access**: Quick creation of lecture sessions with unique join codes and downloadable QR codes for zero-friction student entry.
2. **Live Confusion / Pace Gauge**: Real-time pulse check buttons ("Slower", "Confused", "Got It") allowing students to continuously indicate understanding without interrupting the lecture.
3. **Anonymous Q&A Queue with Upvoting**: Real-time question feed where students submit questions anonymously and upvote questions submitted by peers to surface common misunderstandings.
4. **Lecturer Real-time Dashboard**: Live visual indicators and prioritized question feed designed for effortless scanning while teaching.
5. **Post-Lecture Analytics & Summaries**: Post-session summary reports highlighting peak confusion moments and top answered/unanswered questions for lecture review.

## Success Metrics & UX Principles
- **Zero Friction**: Instant access for students without mandatory account registration or login barriers.
- **Low Latency**: Near-instantaneous synchronization of pulse updates and Q&A votes across all connected clients.
- **Visual Clarity**: Clear, non-distracting visual cues for lecturers to gauge class sentiment at a glance.
